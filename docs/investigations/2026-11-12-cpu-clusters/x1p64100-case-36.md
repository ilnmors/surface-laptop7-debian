# X1P64100: CPU clusters 1+2 get no cpufreq (all SCMI OPPs flagged turbo); surface_platform_profile fails on DT boots

Source: https://github.com/dwhinham/archiso-aarch64-sp11/issues/36

Unit: Surface Pro 11, X1P (X1P-64-100), LCD (LGD 0x077B), Wi-Fi only, UEFI 175.222.235, SAM 27.201.139. Kernel 7.2.8-arch1-3 from your repo, `x1p64100-microsoft-denali.dtb`.

### 1. CPU clusters 1 and 2 get no cpufreq policy

Only `policy0` (cpus 0-3) exists; cpus 4-6 and 8-10 have no `cpufreq/` directory, so 6 of the 10 cores never scale.

`cpufreq_table_validate_and_sort()` returns -EINVAL for the policies of cpu4 and cpu8. The SCMI perf protocol sets `turbo = freq > sustained_freq_khz * 1000`; for perf domains 1 and 2 this firmware apparently reports a sustained frequency of 0, so all 13 OPPs (710.4 to 3417.6 MHz) are registered as turbo and become `CPUFREQ_BOOST_FREQ`. With boost disabled during validation, `cpufreq_frequency_table_cpuinfo()` skips every entry and the policy fails, silently (pr_debug only). Domain 0 reports a sane value and works.

Evidence: a kretprobe on `cpufreq_table_validate_and_sort` returns -22 for cpu4/cpu8; a kprobe on `dev_pm_opp_add_dynamic` shows turbo=1 for all 13 OPPs of both domains.

With the change in the PR (treat a table where *every* entry is boost as regular frequencies), policy4 and policy8 are created and both clusters scale up to 3.4 GHz under load. A cleaner fix might belong in `drivers/firmware/arm_scmi/perf.c` (don't mark OPPs turbo when the sustained frequency is 0), or a firmware quirk; happy to rework it whichever way you prefer.

### 2. `surface_platform_profile` can't load on devicetree boots

`platform_profile_init()` returns -EOPNOTSUPP when `acpi_disabled`, so on the DT boot the SAM device `01:03:01:00:01` stays unbound and power-profiles-daemon only has its placeholder driver. The SP11 registry entry itself is fine (`perf_profile_with_fan`). The PR keeps the class interface on DT boots and puts the legacy `/sys/firmware/acpi/platform_profile` file on its own kobject, because power-profiles-daemon reads that file. Result on this unit: `powerprofilesctl` lists performance/balanced/power-saver with `PlatformDriver: platform_profile`, and the fan profile is sent.

### 3. Cosmetic: X1P64100 DTB has 12 CPU nodes

The X1P64100 is a 10-core part, but the dtb keeps the X1E's cpu@10300 and cpu@20300: `psci: failed to boot CPU7 (-22)` / `CPU11 (-22)`, and they appear in `related_cpus`.

Note: I developed the patches with AI assistance; everything above is tested on my own unit.
